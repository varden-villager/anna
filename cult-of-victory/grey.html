"In intellectual circles, they talk about signs of authoritarianism. Should we worry now? Or wait?
I have my own measure now: Gray-haired twenty-year-olds.

Who return home and visit eight grieving families.
Who see eight fresh graves.
Raise eight glasses — and don’t clink them.
See eight mourning brides.
And pin eight photos above their desk —
And realize there’s no space for anyone else.
There were eight of them.
Now, only one remains. A victim of a war he never fought."

Excerpt from Anna Politkovskaya's article
"The Tenth: You Don’t Know the Worst About the Chechen War" (2000)
